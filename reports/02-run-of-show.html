∙ Part I · Pre-Flight

Before the call connects.

Five minutes of setup so the demo is silent and snappy when it matters.

Setup checklist

  • Run the production build, not the dev server. In the project directory: npm run build && npm run preview. This avoids HMR reloads mid-demo.
  • Open the demo URL five minutes before the call so map tiles cache. Browse all seven screens once to warm everything.
  • Use Safari or Firefox — the in-house Chrome policy blocks localhost on managed machines.
  • Set the country filter to ALL before the call starts. Default theme is Night Mode — keep it there for Screens 01–04, flip to Day Mode for Screen 06.
  • Hide notifications (Do Not Disturb). The toast pattern uses bottom-right corner — your OS toasts will collide visually if they fire.
  • Set zoom to 100%. Layout is tuned for 1440 × 900.

Opening line — pick one.

"I want to show you one shift in the life of a DANX night dispatcher — twenty-two-thirty CET tonight through seven AM tomorrow. Seven screens, ten minutes. By the time we're done you'll know exactly where this product earns its weight."

∙ Part II · The Ten Minutes

Beat by beat.

Each beat is timed for the live cadence. The say lines are the anchor phrases — paraphrase, don't read.

0:00 — 1:30
01

Control Tower — open the network.

Click: Sidebar item 01 (already loaded). Let the map render. Pan slightly toward Sweden.

"It's twenty-two-thirty CET. Eighteen-hundred shipments are moving across seven countries tonight. This is what your dispatcher sees in one screen instead of three browser tabs."

Click one hub marker (Stockholm SE-H01 if visible) to open the drawer. Show the pick rate, cross-dock queue, sortation lanes.

"Warehouse operations live inside the same view — no tool-switching to find out why Stockholm is plus-eighteen."
1:30 — 3:30
02

SLA Risk — the Siemens save.

Click: Sidebar 02. The Siemens MRI shipment is already the hero card.

"This single shipment has eighty-seven percent breach probability. A miss costs Siemens forty-eight-thousand euros an hour. The system saw it coming and pre-staged the intervention."

Walk the six risk factors briefly (point to weather, hub backlog, driver availability). Then click Accept & Propagate on the recommendation card.

"One click. TMS, customer portal, driver app, hub manager — all notified. Thirty seconds, not thirty minutes."
3:30 — 5:00
03

Inventory — vertical swap as theatre.

Click: Sidebar 03. Start on Life Sciences (default vertical).

"The vertical the platform is currently configured for is Life Sciences — four-hour SLA window, forty-eight thousand euro downtime cost. Here's tomorrow's predicted shortage and the move that prevents it."

Click the Auto pill in the vertical row.

"Watch the whole rule profile rewrite. SLA window swaps to eight hours, downtime cost drops to forty-two-hundred euros, handling rules change to line-down priority. Same data, different lens."

Return to Life Sciences. Click Authorise Move on the replenishment card. Show toast.

5:00 — 6:30
04

Route Optimization — the four-second moment.

Click: Sidebar 04. The network is shown undisrupted.

"This is what tonight looks like when the world cooperates. Five hundred routes, planned utilisation."

"E18 just closed between Stockholm and Oslo for three hours. Forty-seven routes recalculated. Cost up six-thousand euros, CO₂ up three-percent, every priority delivery preserved. Four seconds, not ninety minutes of phone calls."

Point briefly at the Fleet & Driver card — utilisation shift, hours-of-service tightening.

∙ Part III · Live Demo Discipline

What to do — and what to avoid.

∙ Do
  • Keep the country filter on ALL for the full walkthrough. Use Sweden only as a story zoom on Screen 01.
  • Pause two seconds after clicking Inject Disruption so the audience catches the red corridor.
  • Use the suggested prompts on Screen 05 — they're scripted to chain.
  • Flip the theme toggle on Screen 06 (or another non-map screen) for a clean visual moment.
  • Anchor every screen with one named customer (Siemens, Volvo Trucks) — proper nouns sell.
  • If a number is questioned, point to the source — every figure on Screen 06 has a footnote on the cover page.
  • Close with the four pilot success metrics — they make the next step concrete.
∙ Don't
  • Don't toggle theme during Screens 01, 04, or 07 — the map / SVG re-render is brief but visible.
  • Don't type free-form into the assistant input on stage — the keyword router covers common topics but isn't ChatGPT.
  • Don't switch the country filter to Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, or Poland before Screen 04. The E18 scenario is anchored to Sweden ↔ Norway.
  • Don't volunteer that the data is mock. Refer to it as "a deterministic snapshot for the demo."
  • Don't promise warehouse, fleet, or executive features that aren't shown — defer them to the pilot scope.
  • Don't run from npm run dev on the call. HMR can reload mid-sentence.
  • Don't apologise for the stub Apply / Schedule buttons. They demonstrate the workflow, not the wiring.

∙ Part IV · Likely Questions

What they'll ask. What to say.

Eight questions from the prospect's CCO, COO and CIO — with the answer you should already have ready.

"Is this live data, or is this a demo?"
"It's a deterministic operational snapshot built off DANX-scale parameters — the seven countries, twenty-nine FSLs, fifty-six hubs and eighty-six PUDOs from your network shape. In the pilot, every figure on these screens comes from your TMS, WMS and telematics feeds."
"Can you show me the warehouse and cross-dock view?"
"Click any hub marker on the Control Tower — pick rate, cross-dock queue, labour active, trailer readiness, sortation lanes. It's contextual to where the dispatcher is already looking. Cut-off countdown and dwell time included."
"How do you track fleet utilisation and driver hours-of-service?"
"On the Route Optimization screen — there's a Fleet & Driver card with utilisation, top-five-driver hours-of-service bars, idle time, cost per shipment, capacity per region. The numbers move when disruption is active so you see the trade-off live."
"Where's the executive dashboard for our CCO?"
"The Customer Report is the externally-shippable view. The internal executive dashboard rolls up the same data model — SLA, exception rate, cost-to-serve, hub performance — into a CCO-facing roll-up. We can stand it up in pilot week two against your real data."
"How does this fit our SAP and our existing TMS / WMS?"
"Screen 07 shows it visually. We don't replace those systems — we listen to them. Event-driven layer on top, eight connectors to start, audit-logged. One pick at twenty-two-thirty-four triggers five downstream updates in under a second."
"What's the implementation timeline?"
"Four-week shaped pilot against one country, one corridor, one anchor customer. Weeks 1–2: integration & data shape. Weeks 3–4: shadow-mode with the night-ops team. Week 5: review and renewal-or-roll. Full network rollout follows the pilot success metrics."
"How do you handle GDPR and customer data residency?"
"Single-tenant deployment with EU-only data residency. Audit log is append-only, replayable. PII (driver names, customer contacts) is encrypted at rest and access-controlled by role. We can walk our DPA in a separate session — happy to send the spec."
"What does this cost?"
"Pilot pricing is fixed-fee — €45k for the four-week shaped pilot, capped. Annual subscription scales by shipment volume tier; for a 1.8M-annual network like yours the indicative range is €240–320k annually, all-in. Final pricing follows the pilot scope."

∙ Part V · Recovery Plays

If something goes sideways.

Three failure modes and the recovery line for each.

The map doesn't load.

Why: CartoDB tile CDN slow or blocked.

What to say: "Network's pulling tiles from a CDN — let me show you the second screen first and we'll come back to the map." Jump to Screen 02. Refresh Screen 01 in the background.

A toast doesn't appear.

Why: Quick double-click or animation interrupted.

What to say: Keep moving. The state change happened. If asked: "Toast is just the confirmation — the action propagated. Watch the queue position drop."

The prospect asks to drive.

Why: Engagement is good — they want to feel it.

What to say: "Absolutely — let me suggest the next screen and you can click around. I'll narrate." Steer them toward Screen 03 (the vertical swap is the safest interactive moment). Avoid the assistant input field.
